In the Heights

Plot guide

Summary (Read Before the Show)

In the Heights takes place in Washington Heights, a Latino neighborhood in New York City where everyone knows each other and forms a close-knit community. The story follows several neighbors whose lives become connected as they face important decisions about their futures, their families, love, identity, and where they truly belong.

The main characters include Usnavi, the owner of a small grocery store who dreams of starting a new life; Sonny, his cousin, who helps him run the store; Vanessa, who hopes to move out and build a better future; Nina, who returns home after her first year at university carrying a secret; Benny, a young worker with big ambitions; Kevin and Camila, Nina’s parents, who want the best for their daughter; and Abuela Claudia, a beloved member of the neighborhood who represents its history and heart. The characters Piraguero and Graffiti Pete also take part in the musical.

Throughout the musical, these characters must make decisions that will test their dreams and their relationships as the community faces unexpected changes. Through music, dance, and humor, In the Heights celebrates the importance of family, friendship, and belonging, showing how a neighborhood can become a true home.

Act by Act Summary

Act I

Scene 1:

Washington Heights – Dawn: Usnavi opens his bodega and introduces the neighborhood while dealing with Graffiti Pete’s graffiti and a broken refrigerator. We meet Abuela Claudia, who raised him and lives by the motto “Patience and Faith,” and the Rosarios, owners of the car service, whose daughter Nina has just returned from college. The central conflict is established: rising rents are forcing local businesses to close or move. Usnavi dreams of returning to the Dominican Republic, while Benny, an employee of the Rosarios who is not Latino, struggles to earn respect at the dispatch.

Scene 2:

On the Street: Nina Rosario returns from Stanford feeling like a failure. In her song “Breathe,” she expresses the pressure of being “the star” of the neighborhood that everyone expects to succeed, while hiding the fact that she has lost her scholarship and dropped out of college.

Scene 3:

Rosario’s Car Service: Kevin and Camila Rosario face serious debts and offers from real estate investors, which Kevin rejects out of pride. Benny runs the dispatch and welcomes Nina; they flirt and remember their childhood, although Benny notices that Nina is under a lot of pressure.

Scene 4:

The Street – Vanessa: Vanessa, who works at Daniela’s beauty salon, dreams of moving to an apartment in the West Village to escape her toxic home and unpaid bills. Usnavi awkwardly tries to ask her out, and they eventually agree to go to a club that night after Nina’s welcome dinner.

Scene 5:

The Car Service Dispatch: Nina finally confesses to her parents that she lost her scholarship because she had to work two jobs to pay for her books, leaving her no time to study. Kevin reacts with a feeling of uselessness (sings “Inútil”), comparing himself to his own father and feeling like a failure for not being able to provide enough for his daughter’s future.

Scene 6:

The Beauty Salon: At Daniela’s salon, the women gossip about the events happening in the neighborhood (sing “No Me Diga”). Tensions rise when Nina reveals to Daniela, Carla, and Vanessa that she has dropped out of college, leaving her friends shocked because she was the only one who had “made it out.”

Scene 7:

Inside the Bodega / The Street: Usnavi discovers that his bodega sold a winning $96,000 lottery ticket. The whole neighborhood joins in a celebration, imagining what they would do with the money: Benny would go to business school, Sonny, Usnavi’s cousin, wants to improve the neighborhood, and Vanessa wants her dream apartment.

Scene 8:

The Street: Abuela Claudia reflects on her journey from Cuba and her life of hard work as a domestic worker in New York. At the end of her song “Paciencia y Fe,” it is revealed that she is the one who has the winning lottery ticket.

Scene 9:

Abuela Claudia’s Apartment Building: Nina and Benny walk together and remember their childhood. Benny assures her that she is still capable of changing the world, and their mutual attraction grows.

Scene 10:

The Street – Sunset: The Piragua Guy celebrates that his business is surviving despite the heat and the competition from “Mister Softee.”

Scene 11:

The Rosario Home: During dinner, Kevin announces that he has sold the family business to pay for Nina’s tuition, without consulting Camila. This causes a family breakdown: Benny is furious about losing his job and being looked down on by Kevin, while Nina refuses to accept that her parents are sacrificing their livelihood for her.

Scene 12:

The Club: Vanessa and Usnavi try to make each other jealous, while Benny and Nina argue over the way Kevin treated Benny. Tensions explode just as a massive blackout occurs.

Scene 13:

The Blackout: A massive blackout hits the city during a hot July night. Amid the chaos and looting, Usnavi and Sonny try to protect the bodega with the help of Graffiti Pete’s fireworks. The scene ends with Nina and Benny finding each other in the darkness and kissing under the fireworks.

Act II

Scene 1:

Benny’s Fire Escape: Nina and Benny have spent the night together. In the song “Sunrise,” Nina teaches Benny Spanish as they reaffirm their relationship and face the uncertainty of what Kevin will say when he sees them together.

Scene 2:

Outside the Bodega: Usnavi finds his store looted and his “first dollar” destroyed. The tension between him and Vanessa increases because of the misunderstandings from the previous night at the club. Meanwhile, Kevin and Camila desperately search for Nina.

Scene 3:

Inside Usnavi and Abuela’s Apartment: Abuela Claudia and Usnavi plan what to do with the lottery money. She wants him to fulfill his dream of returning to the Dominican Republic.

Scene 4:

Outside the Car Service: Camila confronts Kevin and Nina about their stubbornness and constant arguments. In her song “Enough,” she demands that they stop tearing each other apart and remember that family is what matters most, above college or business.

Scene 5:

The Street: Under a scorching heat and with no electricity, the neighborhood is exhausted. Daniela organizes the “Carnaval del Barrio” to lift everyone’s spirits, urging them to proudly wave their national flags. Usnavi officially announces that he will leave for the Dominican Republic the following day.

Scene 6:

The Street – Abuela’s Death: Kevin announces over the radio that Abuela Claudia has died due to the heat and stress.

Scene 7:

The Street: Nina goes through Abuela’s boxes of memories and discovers that she kept every program and newspaper clipping related to Nina’s education. Inspired by this, Nina decides to return to Stanford to finish her degree and honor her family’s sacrifice.

Scene 8:

Outside the Salon: Daniela reveals that she is going to co-sign Vanessa’s new apartment lease, a favor Usnavi secretly asked for and paid for before planning his departure.

Scene 9:

The Bodega: Usnavi gives Sonny his “severance” and prepares to close the bodega. Vanessa arrives and tries to convince him to stay, realizing that he is the heart of the neighborhood and that she loves him.

Scene 10:

The Car Service Dispatch: Benny and Nina say goodbye, promising to maintain their long-distance relationship while she returns to California.

Scene 11:

A Dark Alley: Sonny asks Graffiti Pete for a special and secret favor for the bodega, giving him money for an artistic project.

Scene 12:

Finale: Just as Usnavi is about to leave for the airport, Sonny and Graffiti Pete show him a giant mural of Abuela Claudia painted on the bodega’s metal shutter, featuring the motto “Paciencia y Fe.” Seeing this and looking around, Usnavi realizes that his “island” is not the Caribbean, but Washington Heights, and that his purpose is to stay and tell the stories of his people. He decides to stay and reopen the bodega.
